# Visual asset provenance

`paper-signal.png` is conceptual cover artwork created for this workshop with the built-in image-generation tool on 2026-10-05. It is not a historical photograph, real watermark sample or benchmark result.

Generation prompt:

> Use case: productivity-visual. Create one visually striking premium editorial photograph for a presentation about invisible watermarking. Wide 16:9 composition. An off-white sheet of fibrous handmade paper floats vertically, dramatically folded and curling into a ribbon on the right two-thirds of the frame, lit with a sharp electric chartreuse green grazing light that reveals fine physical paper texture and a faint abstract embossed geometric mark. Matte near-black background. The left third is dark empty negative space for a large typographic title to be added later. Photoreal sculptural paper, analog meets digital, art direction like a contemporary design museum exhibition. Crisp edge lighting, subtle shadow, restrained composition. No words, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no icons, no circuit boards, no holographic sci-fi interface. This is conceptual cover artwork, not scientific evidence.

The StegaStamp video is linked/embedded from the authors' YouTube upload. No YouTube video file is bundled.

## Supplied paper animation

`paper-watermark.gif` is an unchanged copy of the user-supplied `Untitled video.gif`, added on 2026-10-05. It is 640 × 360, with 101 frames at 100 ms each and an infinite-loop flag. `paper-watermark-poster.jpg` is its first frame. The deck labels the clip a conceptual reconstruction and retains the separate historical photograph on the next slide. The source file remains untouched.

## Supplied invisible-ink animations

`invisible-ink-pen.gif` and `invisible-ink-newspaper.gif` are unchanged copies of `UV-Light-Text-1.gif` and `UV-Light-Text-2.gif`, supplied by the user on 2026-10-05. Each is 640 × 360 with 101 frames at 100 ms, looping every 10.1 seconds. The matching `-poster.jpg` files are first frames. Source GIFs remain untouched.

The pen clip illustrates UV-revealed writing. The newspaper clip illustrates a hidden map and is labelled as a conceptual scene, not a historical document. The accompanying [National Archives account of Karl Muller](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/karl-muller-and-the-fatal-lemon/) describes lemon-juice writing revealed with a warm iron. No claim is made that the GIF reproduces that event or its method.

## Expanded real-world cases

- `baker-stage.jpg`: U.S. Army Signal Corps photograph of Josephine Baker on stage in Oran, Algeria, 17 May 1943. [U.S. National Archives, 111-SC-175237 / NAID 531160](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/531160). Public domain. Original photograph, no image alteration.
- `baker-sheet-music.png`: unchanged presenter-supplied Voluptuosa image. [Spy Museum collection context](https://www.spymuseum.org/exhibition-experiences/about-the-collection/collection-highlights/josephine-baker-s-sheet-music/). The museum describes sheet music as the carrier; the deck does not claim a surviving secret message was decoded from this page.
- `genius-watermark.png`: unchanged presenter-supplied Attachment A image. [Genius’s congressional submission](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU05/20190716/109793/HHRG-116-JU05-20190716-SD008.pdf). CSS offers a detail view; the full screenshot remains available.
- `musk-post.png`: unchanged presenter-supplied post screenshot. [Musk’s 9 October 2022 post](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1579101966453858305). CSS enlarges the reply; the whole conversation remains available. The accompanying claim is attributed to Musk.
- `google-bing-query.png` and `google-bing-result.png`: original screenshots from [Google’s 2011 account](https://googleblog.blogspot.com/2011/02/microsofts-bing-uses-google-search.html). The comparison remains qualified by [Microsoft’s response](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2011/2/Setting-the-record-straight/).
- `printer-eff-white-light.jpg`, `printer-eff-blue-light.jpg`, `printer-eff-decoding-guide.png`: unchanged [EFF guide images](https://w2.eff.org/Privacy/printers/docucolor/), originally `faint2.jpg`, `docucolor4.jpg`, and `guide.png`. The guide’s stated sample is 21 June 2005, 12:50, serial 21052857 or 052857. The deck displays that documented interpretation rather than performing image extraction.

Denton’s supplied YouTube upload is embedded and linked, not downloaded. The replay guide gives the documented message and standard Morse spelling, without a claim of frame-level alignment. Historical context is attributed to the U.S. Navy and National Archives.
